1. LockBit Ransomware Breach at Evolve Bank & Trust (2024) 

Company Profile 

Evolve Bank & Trust is a regional bank based in Memphis, Tennessee, known for its banking-as-a-service model partnering with fintech companies. It provides banking services (like accounts and payment processing) to customers of many fintech apps.
Challenge: In May 2024, Evolve Bank & Trust suffered a major ransomware attack by the LockBit cybercriminal group. The attackers managed to breach the bank’s systems (reportedly starting when an employee inadvertently clicked a malicious link, allowing initial access). Once inside, LockBit operatives exfiltrated a large cache of customer data and then encrypted parts of the bank’s network. The breach was massive – Evolve disclosed that the personal data of approximately 7.6 million individuals was compromised. This included names, Social Security numbers, dates of birth, account numbers, and contact information for both Evolve’s direct customers and users of its partner fintech platforms. In some cases, debit card numbers and ACH transaction records (with bank routing and account numbers of payors/payees) were also exposed. 

Response: 
Immediately after discovering systems malfunctioning in late May, the bank initiated incident response protocols and stopped the attack by May 31, 2024. They brought in cybersecurity specialists to investigate and contain the breach. With backups in place, Evolve was able to avoid paying ransom – it began restoring encrypted data from backups and rebuilding affected servers. Critical banking services were restored within a few days, thanks to these recovery efforts. Evolve enhanced its security controls system-wide: it forced a global password reset for all employees and service accounts, rebuilt core identity management systems (like Active Directory) to eliminate any backdoors, and strengthened network defenses by hardening firewalls and deploying advanced endpoint detection and response (EDR) tools across all devices.

Result: within a couple of weeks, banking operations had normalized, and customers could transact, owing to successful data restoration from backups. Evolve faced regulatory investigations and paid fines for the data breach,  the incident prompted Evolve Bank to significantly bolster its security posture. The bank reported that by late 2024, intrusion attempts were being detected and contained far more quickly than before

2.ICBC Financial Services — Ransomware Attack (2023) 

What happened / What went wrong 

On 8 November 2023, ICBC Financial Services (ICBC FS), the U.S. broker-dealer subsidiary of ICBC, discovered that ransomware had encrypted data and programs across its network, leaving employees unable to access critical systems.  

The exact method used by the attackers to initially enter ICBC FS has not been publicly confirmed. Reports suggested exploitation of a Citrix vulnerability. 

The attack affected systems used for fixed-income/repo transactions, equities, and accounting/general-ledger activities.  

Impact 

ICBC FS had to disconnect from clearing firms and clearing agents, disrupting its ability to conduct and settle securities transactions.  ICBC FS reportedly had approximately $9 billion in unsettled Treasury trades that needed to be managed during the disruption. This was not money stolen by the attackers.  The disruption extended into early 2024; this was not simply a few hours of downtime.  This incident created significant reputational and operational risk because a cyberattack on a broker-dealer had consequences beyond the company itself, affecting its ability to interact with other financial-market participants.  

How ICBC responded 

Isolated/disconnected affected systems to contain the attack.  

Reduced operations while systems were being recovered.  

Engaged external cybersecurity specialists to investigate and remediate the incident.  Used manual processes and offline records to continue critical activities.  

What changed afterwards / Lessons learned 
Following the attack, ICBC FS: 
engaged external consultants to identify and address vulnerabilities;  

strengthened its incident-response plans;  

increased security testing and employee cybersecurity training;  
Regulatory consequence 

In December 2024, the SEC censured ICBC FS and issued a cease-and-desist order. The SEC found that the ransomware incident contributed to failures involving books and records and required trade confirmations. 

Importantly, the SEC did not impose a monetary penalty, citing ICBC FS's cooperation and extensive remedial efforts. 

3.  CrowdStrike Outage
CrowdStrike is an endpoint security vendor whose primary technology is the Falcon platform, which helps protect systems against potential threats. It is a flaw in CrowdStrike Falcon that triggered the issue. 
A CrowdStrike update caused a massive IT outage, crashing millions of Windows systems. occurred July 19, 2024, with millions of Windows systems failing and showing the infamous blue screen of death (BSOD). Insurers estimate the outage will cost U.S. Fortune 500 companies $5.4 billion.

What caused the outage? 

The CrowdStrike Falcon platform is widely used by organizations of all sizes across many industries and its integration into so many mission-critical operations and industries that amplified the effect. Falcon hooks into the Microsoft Windows OS as a Windows kernel process. The process has high privileges, giving Falcon the ability to monitor operations in real time across the OS.
The flaw in CrowdStrike Falcon was inside of a sensor configuration update. The sensor is regularly updated -- sometimes multiple times daily.
However on this fateful day,  CrowdStrike inadvertently introduced a logic error, causing the Falcon sensor to crash and, subsequently, Windows systems in which it was integrated.

Impact
Microsoft estimated that approximately 8.5 million Windows devices were directly affected by the CrowdStrike logic error flaw.  the systems affected were those running critical operations. Services affected include the following. 
Airlines and airports , Healthcare, Financial Services.
Lawsuits followed 
Delta Air Lines filed a lawsuit against CrowdStrike on Oct. 25, 2024, over the outage that grounded thousands of flights and cost the airline approximately $500 million.

The Response  
 CrowdStrike itself was able to identify and deploy a fix for the issue in 79 minutes. While CrowdStrike quickly identified and deployed a fix for the issue, the recovery process for businesses is complex and time-consuming. once the problematic update was installed, the underlying Windows OS would trigger BSOD, rendering the system inoperative using the normal boot process. 

IT administrators had to manually boot affected systems into Safe Mode or the Windows Recovery Environment to delete the problematic channel file 291 and restore normal operations. In some cases, the process also required physical access to each machine, adding further time and effort to the process. As of July 29, 2024, CrowdStrike reported that approximately 99% of affected Windows sensors were back online. 

What changed afterwards? Adam Meyers testified before the U.S House Subcommittee on Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Protection. Updates are now treated like code updates, with internal testing and phased implementation. 

A new "system of concentric rings" approach for rolling out updates has been implemented. 

Customers can now choose their level of update adoption: early adopter, general availability or opt-out/delay. 

Hackers take advantage of outage:
Crowdstrike received reports of the following malicious activity: 

Phishing emails sent to customers posing as CrowdStrike support. 

Fake phone calls impersonating CrowdStrike staff. 

Selling scripts claiming to automate recovery from the botched update.
